# Research Report: Delhi NCR Hotel Recommendation Dataset Visualization & Analysis Summary

This report summarizes the exploratory data analysis (EDA) findings from the 8 Jupyter notebooks in the **Stage 11.75 Research Visualization Notebook Suite**. These notebooks analyze hotel metadata, text reviews, NLP sentiment scores, aspect-based sentiment scores (ABSA), engineered scores, user preference distributions, and interaction sequences.

---

## 1. Key Findings

1. **Geographic Clustering**: Hotels are heavily clustered around central New Delhi (Paharganj and Karol Bagh), the Indira Gandhi International Airport corridor (Mahipalpur), and the commercial hub of Gurugram. The spatial distribution has strong recommendation utility but requires geographic clustering to prevent recommending distant locations.
2. **Review Positivity Bias**: Review ratings are extremely positive (median rating is 4.10, and individual review ratings are heavily skewed toward 5 stars). Negative reviews (1-2 stars) are highly sparse.
3. **Sentiment Consistency**: NLP positive sentiment probabilities generated by our DistilBERT model are highly correlated (~0.84 Pearson coefficient) with star ratings. This indicates high model classification accuracy on review text.
4. **Cleanliness Differentiation**: Among the 5 extracted aspect sentiment scores (Cleanliness, Service, Location, Value for Money, Staff Behavior), **Cleanliness** has the highest variance. Cleanliness is a key differentiator among Delhi NCR hotels.
5. **Independent Popularity and Trust**: Popularity scores (derived from review volume) are highly skewed and follow a power-law distribution. Trust scores (combining rating, sentiment, and review volume) are normally distributed around 0.68. There is a low correlation between popularity and trust, proving they represent independent evaluation signals.
6. **Synthetic Interaction Alignment**: Synthetic users interact (clicks, bookings, reviews) in a pattern showing moderate-to-high overlap with their demographic preferences (budget match rate: ~66%, area match rate: ~51%), demonstrating that the collaborative filtering and content filtering signals are successfully injected.

---

## 2. Dataset Strengths

* **High Review Coverage**: Out of 1,661 unique hotels, **1,618 (97.4%)** have text reviews and valid aspect sentiment extractions. This minimized cold-start issues for content-based models.
* **Rich Granular Features**: The aspect scores (Cleanliness, Service, Location, Value for Money, Staff Behavior) enable multi-dimensional explainability and personalized recommendation matching.
* **Coherent NLP Signals**: Strong correlation between review ratings and sentiment probabilities validates the NLP extraction quality.
* **Structured Evaluation Target**: The synthetic interaction dataset (5,000 interactions across 500 users) provides a clear benchmark target for testing collaborative and hybrid recommenders.

---

## 3. Dataset Weaknesses

* **Missing API Pricing Data**: The Google Places API returned `price_level` as 100% missing (NaN) for all 1,661 hotels. To mitigate this, we engineered a `budget_category` proxy feature based on area characteristics and rating metrics.
* **Truncated Review Volume**: Raw reviews are capped at 5 per hotel due to Google Places API detail request limits. This prevents us from capturing long-tail review volumes for top-tier hotels.
* **Synthetic Demographics**: User preferences and interaction patterns are synthetic. While modeled on realistic distributions, they may lack the subtle behavioral noise of real user journeys.

---

## 4. Potential Biases

* **Positivity Skew**: The extreme concentration of 5-star ratings could bias recommendations toward high-rated options, overshadowing subtle aspect differentiators.
* **Geographic Skew**: Over 57% of hotels are concentrated in New Delhi, meaning recommendations could be geographically skewed toward New Delhi without location filters.

---

## 5. Outliers and Anomalies

* **Geographic Coordinates**: 2 hotels have coordinates outside the primary bounding box of Delhi NCR (which were cleaned/removed in prior phases), but a few hotels still lie at the fringe boundaries of Noida/Ghaziabad.
* **Sparsity**: A small subset of 43 hotels have 0 reviews, resulting in NaN values for their aspect sentiment scores and engineered metrics.
* **Active Users**: A few synthetic user profiles show disproportionately high booking rates relative to clicks, which needs to be handled via interaction normalization.

---

## 6. Recommendations Before Recommendation-Model Development

1. **Aspect Score Imputation**: Before computing content similarities, impute the NaN aspect scores (for the 43 hotels with no reviews) using the median score of their `area_cluster` or the overall dataset median.
2. **Explicit Interaction Weighting**: In Stage A, model user-hotel interaction types (`view`, `click`, `booking`, `review`) using weighted values (e.g. Booking = 5.0, Click = 1.0, View = 0.5) to capture implicit feedback strength.
3. **Address Positivity Bias**: Normalise ratings or center-scale review ratings to emphasize negative feedback.
4. **Geographic Filtering Constraint**: Implement a hard constraint or similarity penalty for distance in the hybrid recommender so that recommended hotels match the user's preferred area cluster.
